पताक patāka
“the beginning of a dance”

A hand, opening

Scroll, and the gesture changes. This is the arc of a dancer here — told in the only vocabulary that can tell it properly.

पताक patāka — “the beginning of a dance”

It starts with a flag

Every performance opens with patāka — the flat, open hand. Nothing has happened yet. Everything is about to.

A child arrives at four or five, usually holding a parent’s hand, and is taught first how to stand and how to greet the floor she will dance on.

मुकुल mukula — “a water lily”

Closed, and full of it

Five fingertips gathered to a point. Mukula is a bud — a water lily before light gets to it.

The first year is shlokas, rhythm and play. Very little looks like dancing yet. Everything that comes later is being folded in.

पद्मकोश padmakośa — “a lotus bud”

The first opening

Padmakośa is the same bud with air inside it — fingers spread and cupped, holding a shape without closing on it.

Levels one and two. Adavus become reliable, rhythm becomes countable, and a student begins to notice she is being understood when she moves.

अलपद्म alapadma — “a full-bloomed lotus”

Full bloom

Alapadma. Every finger turned out, nothing held back. It is the gesture sitting inside the academy’s own logo, in the pair of open hands.

It also means a mirror, the full moon, a village, a lake, and unmistakable anger. By now a dancer can make one hand say any of them, and an audience will know which.

हंसपक्ष haṃsapakṣa — “to denote the number six”

Six levels, counted on one hand

Haṃsapakṣa — the swan’s wing — is the gesture used to say six. The Gurukulam has six levels above its introductory stage.

Alarippu and Jatiswaram at three. Keerthanam and Thillana at four. Varnam and Padam at five. At six, a dancer is being prepared for a stage of her own.

हंसास्य haṃsāsya — “giving instruction”

And then she teaches

The same swan, its face this time. Haṃsāsya is the gesture for giving instruction — thumb and two fingers meeting, the shape of a point being made carefully.

Aparna Manu was the first student here. She now leads the Delhi branch. Kirusanthini trained under Sunitta for seven years and now teaches in Scarborough. The line closes.

भ्रमर bhramara — “wings”

Udaan

Bhramara is the bee, and it is also, simply, wings.

Udaan means flying high to reach our goals. It is what the annual production is called, and it is the last thing this sequence has to say.

हंसपक्ष haṃsapakṣa
“writing a letter with the nails”

Start at the beginning

Ages four and up, in Chennai, Delhi and Scarborough.
